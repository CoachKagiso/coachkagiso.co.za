# Audio Script: Tier 2, Mid-Career Panel Simulation

**Product:** Premium tier asset, Interview Story Bank Workbook
**Tier:** 2 of 3 (mid-career)
**Target runtime:** 26 minutes
**Spoken content:** approximately 7 minutes
**Silence:** approximately 19 minutes
**Version:** Draft 1, 4 August 2026

---

## Cast

| Character | Role | Voiced by | Register |
|---|---|---|---|
| **THANDIWE MOKOENA** | Operations Manager. Chair of the panel. The hiring manager. | Kagiso | Warm, efficient, slightly brisk. Genuinely friendly in the warm-up, noticeably more neutral once the questions start. |
| **RIAAN DE BEER** | HR Business Partner | Second voice | Procedural, pleasant, flat. Not cold, just administrative. |
| **NOMSA DLAMINI** | Finance Manager, cross-functional panellist | Third voice | Almost silent. Speaks twice in the entire recording. When she does, she is direct and unhurried. |

**Production note on company and role:** no company name and no job title are used anywhere in this script. This is deliberate. The listener substitutes their own. Any reference to the work is kept generic enough to carry across sectors.

**Production note on silence:** every silence marker below is real recorded room tone, not digital silence. Quiet room, occasional paper movement, one chair creak somewhere in the middle. Never a clean digital gap.

---

## SEGMENT 1: INSTRUCTIONS

*Kagiso, out of character. Direct to listener. Warm, unhurried.*

**KAGISO:**
Before we start, four things.

This runs in real time. Do not pause it. When the panel stops talking, that silence is yours. Answer out loud, into the room, at full volume, as though they can hear you.

The silences are long on purpose. If you finish early, sit in the silence. Do not fill it. Learning to stop talking is half of what we are practising here.

Have your story cards next to you for your first run through. Put them away for your second.

And record yourself on your phone while this plays. You will not enjoy listening back. Do it anyway. That recording is the actual homework.

One more thing. Nobody is listening to you right now. That is the point. You get to be bad at this in private, before it costs you anything.

Right. Take a breath. Here we go.

*[PAUSE: 3 seconds]*

*[Room tone begins. A door. Chairs.]*

**THANDIWE:**
Come in, come in. Thanks for making the time.

Have a seat, wherever you're comfortable.

*[PAUSE: 2 seconds]*

Can we get you something to drink before we start? We've got water, there's tea and coffee, or a cold drink if you'd prefer.

*[SILENCE: 5 seconds, room tone]*

**THANDIWE:**
Great. Let me introduce everyone quickly.

I'm Thandiwe Mokoena, I head up operations, and this role would report directly to me.

**RIAAN:**
Riaan de Beer, HR Business Partner. I'll come in with a few questions later on, mostly around process and package.

**NOMSA:**
Nomsa Dlamini, Finance. I'll mostly just be listening today.

**THANDIWE:**
So, how this will work. We've got about thirty minutes. Most of my questions are competency-based, which means I'll be asking you for specific examples from your own experience rather than what you'd do hypothetically. Take your time with them.

You'll notice all three of us taking notes. Please don't read anything into that, it's just how we score.

And we'll leave time at the end for any questions you've got for us.

*[PAUSE: 1 second]*

Before we get into it, did you find us alright?

*[SILENCE: 45 seconds, room tone]*

## SEGMENT 3: OPENING QUESTION

**THANDIWE:**
Good. Let's start broadly then.

Take us through your background, and what brought you to this role.

*[SILENCE: 100 seconds, room tone]*

**THANDIWE:**
Thank you.

## SEGMENT 4: COMPETENCY BLOCK ONE

### Question 1: Delivering under pressure

**THANDIWE:**
I'd like to start with delivery.

Tell me about a time you had to deliver something with less than you needed. Less time, less budget, fewer people, whatever the constraint was. Walk me through what you actually did.

*[SILENCE: 100 seconds, room tone]*

### PROBE 1: Attribution

**THANDIWE:**
Thanks. Just so I've got this clear in my notes.

You said "we" a few times there. What was your part, specifically?

*[SILENCE: 60 seconds, room tone]*

**THANDIWE:**
Got it.

### Question 2: Conflict or difficult stakeholder

**THANDIWE:**
Next one.

Think of someone you've had to work with who made your job harder. A colleague, a manager, a supplier, a client, it doesn't matter who.

Tell me about a specific occasion with that person, and what you did about it.

*[SILENCE: 100 seconds, room tone]*

**THANDIWE:**
Thank you.

### Question 3: Influence without authority

**THANDIWE:**
Now something slightly different.

Tell me about a time you needed someone to do something, and you had no authority over them at all. They didn't report to you, and you couldn't escalate.

How did you get it done?

*[SILENCE: 100 seconds, room tone]*

**THANDIWE:**
Right. Riaan, do you want to come in here?

## SEGMENT 5: HR BLOCK

**RIAAN:**
Thanks Thandiwe. Yes, a few from my side, and these are more on the practical side.

*[PAUSE: 1 second]*

First one. Why are you leaving your current role? Or, if you've already left, what happened there?

*[SILENCE: 100 seconds, room tone]*

**RIAAN:**
Thank you.

Looking at your CV, I can see there's a period there that I'd like to understand better. Could you talk me through that gap, and what you were doing during that time?

*[SILENCE: 100 seconds, room tone]*

**RIAAN:**
Appreciate that.

Now, on package. What are you currently earning, and what are your expectations for this role?

*[SILENCE: 100 seconds, room tone]*

**RIAAN:**
Noted, thank you.

And last one from me for now. If we were to make an offer, what's your notice period, and when would you realistically be able to start?

*[SILENCE: 45 seconds, room tone]*

**RIAAN:**
That's helpful. Thandiwe, back to you.

## SEGMENT 6: COMPETENCY BLOCK TWO

### Question 4: Process improvement

**THANDIWE:**
Thanks Riaan.

Tell me about something in your work that wasn't working properly, that nobody had asked you to fix, and you fixed it anyway.

*[SILENCE: 100 seconds, room tone]*

### PROBE 2: Evidence

**THANDIWE:**
Is there a number attached to that?

*[SILENCE: 60 seconds, room tone]*

**THANDIWE:**
Thank you.

### Question 5: Failure and feedback

**THANDIWE:**
This next one is the uncomfortable one, so take your time.

Tell me about something you got wrong. A decision that didn't work, a project that failed, or a piece of feedback that landed hard.

What happened, and what did you do afterwards?

*[SILENCE: 100 seconds, room tone]*

**THANDIWE:**
Thank you for being straight about that.

### Question 6: Adaptability

**THANDIWE:**
Last one from me.

Tell me about a time everything changed underneath you. New system, new structure, new leadership, priorities reversed, something along those lines.

What did that require from you?

*[SILENCE: 100 seconds, room tone]*

**THANDIWE:**
Thank you.

*[PAUSE: 2 seconds]*

Nomsa, anything from your side?

## SEGMENT 7: THE OBSERVER

*[PAUSE: 2 seconds. Nomsa has not spoken since the introductions, roughly twenty minutes ago. She is unhurried.]*

**NOMSA:**
Yes, thank you Thandiwe. Just one.

*[PAUSE: 1 second]*

You've told us quite a lot about what you did in these situations.

I'd like to hear about what you decided not to do. Pick any one of those examples. What was the option you considered and rejected, and why did you reject it?

*[SILENCE: 100 seconds, room tone]*

**NOMSA:**
Thank you. That's all from me.

**THANDIWE:**

**THANDIWE:**
So, next steps.

We're seeing a few more people this week and early next week. Riaan will come back to you by the end of next week either way, and if we take it further there would be one more conversation, probably with my director.

**RIAAN:**
Thanks very much.

**NOMSA:**
Thank you.

**THANDIWE:**
Take care. Riaan will walk you out.

*[SILENCE: 3 seconds, clean]*

---

## SEGMENT 10: DEBRIEF

*Kagiso, out of character. Slower than the instructions at the top. Let the listener come down.*

**KAGISO:**
Okay. That's it. Breathe.

*[PAUSE: 2 seconds]*

Four things to do now, while it's still fresh.

One. Play back your own recording. Not tomorrow. Now, while you still remember how each answer felt while you were giving it.

Two. Score it against the audit checklist in your workbook. Six questions. Did you give a specific incident, or did you describe how you generally do things? Did you say "I did", or did you hide behind "we"? Was there a result? Was there a number, or an honest stand-in for one? Did you connect it to what the employer needs? And did you land it in under two minutes?

Three. Find the question you handled worst. Not the one you liked least. The one where the recording is genuinely weak. That's your next story card. Go and build it properly.

*[PAUSE: 1 second]*

Four, and this one matters. Notice what happened at the probes. When Thandiwe asked what your part was, specifically. When she asked if there was a number. If those two moments knocked you off balance, you're not unusual. That is where most people lose the interview, and it's the whole reason those probes are in here.

*[PAUSE: 2 seconds]*

Run this again in three days. Not tomorrow. You need time to build the story you just discovered was missing.

You'll be better on the second run. Everyone is.

*[END]*

## Notes for the other two tiers

Tier 1 and Tier 3 use this exact skeleton. Only three things change:

1. **The six competency questions.** Tier 1 moves to teamwork, learning under pressure, problem-solving, dependability, communication and resilience, and Thandiwe adds one line early on giving explicit permission to use academic, campus, volunteer or informal-work examples. Tier 3 moves to strategic decisions with incomplete information, leading through change, a difficult people decision, senior stakeholder conflict, a decision that did not work, and building capability.
2. **The HR block.** Tier 1 drops the gap question and adds a question about why this field. Tier 3 keeps the gap question and adds one on why they are moving at this stage of their career.
3. **Nomsa's question.** Tier 1 gets the self-awareness version. Tier 3 gets the accountability version. Both are in section 5 of the structure document.

Everything else, including the warm-up, the probes, the close and the debrief, stays word for word.
